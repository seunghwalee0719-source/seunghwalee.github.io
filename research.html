Research

From sustainable reactions to working interfaces and electrochemical systems.

SEE Lab connects electrocatalysis, operando interfacial analysis, and system engineering to understand electrochemical reactions under realistic operating conditions and translate fundamental insight into sustainable energy technologies.

Research Framework

Three connected research pillars.

Our research spans molecular-scale reaction chemistry, real-time observation of active interfaces, and the engineering of electrochemical devices and systems.

01
Reaction

Green Hydrogen & Sustainable Reactions

We develop catalysts and electrochemical pathways that convert renewable electricity, water, carbon-containing feedstocks, and other molecules into hydrogen, fuels, and value-added chemicals.

Hydrogen

Water Electrolysis

Catalyst design for oxygen and hydrogen evolution, with emphasis on activity, durability, materials utilization, and operation at practically relevant current densities.

Carbon Electrochemistry

CO₂ Conversion

Electrochemical conversion of CO₂ toward C1 and multi-carbon products through control of catalyst composition, surface structure, and reaction intermediates.

Sustainable Chemistry

Electrochemical Synthesis

Alternative electrochemical reaction routes for producing fuels and chemicals, including carbon- and nitrogen-based electrochemical transformations.

02
Analysis

Operando Interfacial Analysis

Catalysts are dynamic under bias. We observe working interfaces in real time to identify reaction intermediates, active phases, interfacial structures, and mechanistic descriptors that cannot be captured by ex-situ characterization alone.

Signal
Interface

See the interface while it works.

We build and apply in-house operando/in-situ platforms to correlate spectral signatures with electrochemical performance and reveal the origin of activity, selectivity, and degradation.

03
System

Electrochemical Systems

We extend catalyst-level understanding toward cells, reactors, and devices by integrating materials, transport, interface monitoring, and electrochemical engineering.

Water Electrolysis

Electrolyzer Engineering

Cell design, catalyst loading, operating-condition optimization, scale-up, and translation toward AEM/alkaline and PEM water-electrolysis systems.

Energy Conversion

Fuel-Cell Interfaces

Interface-sensitive evaluation and monitoring of hydrogen oxidation, water structure, electrode environments, and performance under device-relevant conditions.

Enabling Tools

Cells & Monitoring Platforms

Development of spectroelectrochemical cells, accessories, monitoring protocols, and interface-analysis platforms for electrochemical and battery-related systems.
